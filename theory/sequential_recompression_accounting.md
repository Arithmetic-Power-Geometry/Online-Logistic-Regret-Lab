# Logit perturbation and the compensation barrier

Write binary logistic/log loss using logit z:
  ell(z,y)=A(z)-y z,
where y in {0,1} and
  A(z)=log(1+exp(z)),
  p=A'(z)=sigma(z).

Let reference logit z and approximate logit z+delta. Then exactly

  ell(z+delta,y)-ell(z,y)
   = A(z+delta)-A(z)-y delta
   = (p-y) delta + D_A(z+delta,z),

where
  D_A(z+delta,z)
   = A(z+delta)-A(z)-A'(z)delta
is the Bregman divergence of A and is nonnegative/quadratic locally.

Thus the approximation tax splits EXACTLY into

  FIRST ORDER: (p-y) delta
  SECOND ORDER: D_A >= 0.

Since A''(z)=p(1-p)<=1/4,
  D_A(z+delta,z) <= delta^2/8
globally by smoothness.

Therefore

  ell(z+delta,y)-ell(z,y)
  <= (p-y)delta + delta^2/8.

## What must telescope

The only obstacle to a quadratic tax is now the signed term

  sum_t (p_t-y_t) delta_t.

A compensated algorithm needs an account Phi_t satisfying approximately

  (p_t-y_t)delta_t <= Phi_t-Phi_{t+1} + controllable quadratic terms.

## Can ordinary posterior approximation provide this automatically?

For exact Bayesian/EW prediction, p_t is a posterior predictive probability.
After observing y_t, the exact Bayesian log normalizer changes by exactly the
log predictive probability of y_t.

An approximate posterior has its own normalizer and its own prediction q_t.
The cumulative difference of their log losses DOES telescope to the
difference between their sequential marginal likelihoods if each approximate
posterior is updated EXACTLY from its previous approximate posterior.

However, if we RECOMPRESS after each update, an extra recompression
normalizer/error term appears.

This suggests a better accounting unit:

  not per-round |q_t-p_t|,
  but per-round RECOMPRESSION divergence.

## Candidate recompression theorem

Let Q_t be an approximate posterior state before round t.
1. Predict q_t exactly from Q_t.
2. After y_t, perform the exact Bayes logistic update to obtain Q_t^+.
3. Compress Q_t^+ to Q_{t+1}.

Then the learner's cumulative log loss equals the negative log marginal
likelihood generated by the uncompressed transitions, while the cost of
compression may be bounded by a divergence/potential between Q_t^+ and
Q_{t+1}.

If compression is designed as a projection minimizing KL or another Bregman
divergence, the first-order term may be absorbed by a Pythagorean/projection
identity.

This is fundamentally different from independently approximating the exact EW
probability p_t each round.

## Why this route could weaken t^-2

If cumulative approximation tax is controlled by
  sum_t D(Q_t^+ || Q_{t+1})
or a telescoping potential, each compression step might only need total
divergence O(log T), rather than prediction error O(t^-2).

That could make fixed/polylog representation feasible.

## Critical caveat

A generic KL-small compression does not automatically upper-bound adversarial
future log loss, because log loss is unbounded near probabilities 0/1.
The projection family and divergence direction matter.
