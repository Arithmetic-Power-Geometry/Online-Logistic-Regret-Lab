# Score Nullity and Predictive Identifiability

## Setup

Let the Gaussian prior be N(0,s^2 I_d). Suppose observed covariates belong to a
finite dictionary v_1,...,v_r in R^d. For direction j define counts n_j^+,
n_j^-, n_j=n_j^++n_j^- and posterior predictive probability

  p_j = E[sigma(theta^T v_j)].

Let mu=E[theta] under the exact posterior and V=[v_1 ... v_r].

The expected posterior score identity is

  V a = mu/s^2,

where

  a_j = n_j^+ - n_j p_j.

## Definition: Score Nullity

Define

  SN(V) = dim ker(V) = r-rank(V).

This is the number of predictive degrees of freedom left unresolved by the
first posterior-score identity.

## Proposition 1: zero-nullity identifiability

If SN(V)=0 (equivalently V has full column rank), then a is unique:

  a = V^+ mu/s^2,

and every p_j with n_j>0 is exactly determined by

  p_j = [n_j^+-(V^+mu/s^2)_j]/n_j.

Thus exact EW predictions on the observed dictionary are functions only of
posterior mean, counts, and V.

## Proposition 2: positive-nullity algebraic ambiguity

If SN(V)>0 and a_0 is one solution of

  V a = mu/s^2,

then every

  a=a_0+z,  z in ker(V),

also satisfies the same first-score equation.

Correspondingly,

  p_j(z) = [n_j^+-a_{0,j}-z_j]/n_j.

Therefore the first score identity plus posterior mean and counts does not
algebraically determine the predictive vector whenever a nonzero nullspace
perturbation remains compatible with 0<p_j(z)<1.

This is a statement about insufficiency of FIRST-SCORE INFORMATION. It is not
yet an impossibility theorem for all compact posterior summaries: higher score
identities or other moments may resolve z.

## New research question: nullspace closure

Can the nullspace component be recovered cheaply from a hierarchy of posterior
score/Stein identities whose required order is polynomial in d but independent
of T and B?

If yes, this could lead to a compact surrogate for EW prediction.
If no, an adversary may continually create new score-null directions, yielding
an information/computation obstruction.

## Candidate hierarchy

For smooth vector test functions phi(theta), Stein's identity gives

  E[div phi(theta) + phi(theta)^T grad log q(theta)] = 0.

Choosing phi to be low-degree functions of projections theta^T v_j creates
higher-order linear/nonlinear constraints on predictive expectations.

Define Score Closure Order SCO(V,D) as the smallest order k for which score
identities generated by test functions of degree <=k uniquely determine the
needed next-round predictive expectations to tolerance D^{-1} (or another
specified accuracy).

This is only a research definition at present.

## Falsification targets

1. Construct d=1,r=2 dependent dictionaries and show first-score ambiguity.
2. Check whether the exact logistic structure plus a single additional second
   Stein identity closes that ambiguity.
3. Increase r and search whether required closure order grows with r.
4. If closure order grows with T under adversarial direction generation, this
   route cannot yield a fixed-dimensional O(poly(d)) state.
5. If closure order is bounded by poly(d), implement the resulting predictor
   and attack its regret.

The immediate novelty candidate is not the classical Stein identity itself.
It is the use of score-nullity/closure order as a structural measure of how
much posterior predictive information is missing from computationally cheap
moment summaries in online logistic regression.
